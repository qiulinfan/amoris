# Windward

A soaring arcade game. You fly a silk-and-wood glider along an old wind road strung between
floating islands above a sea of sunset cloud. Ten stone wind gates hang between the islands; you
thread them in order, trade height for speed and speed for height, catch the broad thermals and
the gust embers, and try to reach the last gate before the light runs out. The wind's own rider,
a translucent blue glider, paces the same road; beat it if you can. The run is about a minute,
with a saved best time and a medal.

The scene, the islands, the gates, the trees, the clouds, the birds and the glider are generated
by the scripts in this folder; every texture and mesh is original.

## Playing

From the engine's repository root:

```sh
cargo run --release -p amoris-app -- play samples/windward --walk
```

Export the browser edition (WebGPU, keyboard and mouse):

```sh
cargo xtask export-web samples/windward --out out/windward-web --zip out/windward-web.zip
```

Serve the exported folder on a static host, or upload its zip as an itch.io HTML5 game. The browser
keeps the chase camera, holds the starting line until the first click, and pauses when the mouse is
released. It has no audio or persistent best-time save.
In an embedded browser that refuses mouse capture, clicking starts keyboard play with drag-to-look;
Escape or losing window focus pauses it.

Click to capture the mouse.

- **A / D** bank and turn left and right.
- **W / S** dive and climb. Diving gains speed, climbing spends it; the wing sinks slowly when
  level. Keep enough speed to stay flying.
- **Shift** holds a gust: it burns the gust charge and pushes the glider forward.
  **Space** (or the left mouse button) fires a short, stronger burst.
- **Mouse** looks around; the view springs back behind the wing.
- **Right mouse button** starts the run again.
- **Escape** releases the mouse; Escape again exits.

The heads-up display names the next gate and its distance, the light left, the gates passed, the
best run, the rival's gate and the gap to it, the gust charge, the airspeed, the height and the
wind. The golden crystal above a gate
is the next one; a gate you have passed keeps a golden ring. A red **WIPEOUT** message means the
glider met rock or cloud; it is put back on the road a little before the gate it last passed.
When the light reaches zero, the run ends and right-click starts another.

- Ten gates, in order. Passing one adds six seconds of light and a little gust charge.
- Ten gust embers refill the charge and add two seconds.
- The thermals (columns of motes and circling birds) lift the glider; the stronger the lift, the
  closer to their core. The speed line says **LIFT** while it happens.
- A medal at the end: gold under 1:00, silver under 1:30, bronze otherwise. The best time is kept
  for the session.
- The rival follows the gates on its own line, a few metres above their centres. If it reaches the
  last gate first, the result says so; the medal still comes from your time. The top-right line
  shows the rival's gate count and how far ahead or behind it is.

The `player` seat perceives the light left, gates, speed, height, heading, pitch, its own position,
wipeout state, gust charge, best time, medal and
the events `wind.gate`, `wind.ember`, `wind.crash`, `wind.finish` and `wind.fail`. It also sees the
beacon, the gates on the road with their order and the rival with the gates it has passed, so an
agent can fly the run from its own perception. The glider is the seat: its `Walker` receives the window's keys and its `Observer` owns those instruments, while the
game's rules fly it and pose the separate chase camera (`camera = "game"` in `project.toml`).

## How it is built

- `scripts/main.ts` is the game: the flight model (bank, pitch, speed, sink, stall), the wind and
  thermals, the clock, gates, embers, wipeouts and respawns, the pass rings, the burst and its
  trail, the birds, the beacon, the ribbons, the chase camera and the HUD. All state lives in the
  game's own components (`Flight`, `Course`, `Gate`, `Ember`, `Island`, `Thermal`, `Cam`, `Ribbon`,
  `Bird`, `Pass`, `Trail`, `Rival`, `Beacon`, `Hud`), so a snapshot or a replay carries the run
  with it. The rival's line is a Catmull-Rom path through the launch and the gate centres; it
  advances by distance at a fixed pace, and the result compares the two finish times.
- `build_scene.py` writes `scene.json` and `perception.json`, and calls the generators for the
  islands, props and gates. It also draws the run's 20 islands (three silhouettes scaled and
  turned), the wind gates, the ruins, the cloud puffs, the horizon bank and the route's embers
  and thermals. It also adds the blue rival glider and its mist trail.
- `terrain.py` builds the floating islands: a grass plateau over a jagged rock cone, with the
  rock and grass textures generated by `textures.py` and embedded in each `.glb`.
- `props.py` builds the pines, the boulders and hanging prongs, the cluster clouds and the cloud
  sea, the birds, the beacon crystal, the pass ring, the stone arch, the old pillars and the
  cloth ribbon mesh. `gltf.py` is the small glTF writer they share.
- `build_glider.py` builds the glider in Blender (Blender 4.5, headless) and exports
  `glider.glb`: a curved coral and cream sail split into three material meshes, wooden spars, a
  prone pilot with a scarf, a lantern, a tail fin and wingtip fins. Run it with
  `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --python samples/windward/build_glider.py`;
  `--preview` after `--` also renders an ignored preview under `out/agent-games/b/`.
- `check.toml` runs the project's determinism, fork, replay and reload checks; `cargo xtask check`
  runs it with the rest of the samples, or `amoris check samples/windward` runs it alone.

Regenerate everything from the generators:

```sh
python3 samples/windward/build_scene.py
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --python samples/windward/build_glider.py
```

The game uses one engine option added for it: `[player] camera = "game"` lets a first-person
game's rules pose the window's active camera instead of the mouse's look, while the seat still
receives the mouse and keys. The default, `"look"`, is unchanged. The glider's flight and the
chase camera use only the `amoris` module and project files.
