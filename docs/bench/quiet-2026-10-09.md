# Benchmark methods and final choices — 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-10

These tables summarize the last reliable Pioneer measurements on Windows R1. They establish
workload-specific choices and limits; they do not validate a later Amoris build or measure Apple
performance. Detailed run receipts, process inventories, captures and logs belong in local
ignored output. Individual benchmark documents retain the method and useful final tables.

## Machine and measurement method

| Item | Condition |
|---|---|
| Host | ASUS ROG Zephyrus G14, Ryzen 9 270, 16 threads, 15 GB RAM |
| OS | Windows 11 Pro 10.0.26200 |
| Discrete GPU | RTX 5060 Laptop, 8 GB, driver 32.0.16.1714 / NVIDIA 617.14 |
| Integrated GPU | Radeon 780M, driver 32.0.13062.3005 |
| Toolchain | Rust 1.98.1, wgpu 30.0.1, DXC 1.8.2502, Windows SDK 10.0.26100 |
| Browser | Chrome 155.0.8059.39, WebGPU through D3D12 |
| Power and background | AC, Balanced plan; no competing agent builds; desktop GUI load remained |

Idle CPU load was 4–13% between suites. This is a quieter machine, not an unloaded one.
Builds completed before measurements. A/B configurations interleaved, with at least three rounds
and five where practical. Tables use the rounds' median, with minimum and spread where useful;
failed configurations are errors, not zero-cost samples. No best-run selection is used.

GPU time sums the stated timestamped passes. Wall time includes submission and waiting offscreen,
presented-frame intervals in a window, or requestAnimationFrame intervals in Chrome. Those metrics
are not interchangeable. Each suite pins AA, AO and occlusion settings or states the defaults used.

| Suite | Repeats | Startup settle | RTX temperature control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final backend comparison | Five headless, three windowed | 5 s | At most 70°C before each round |
| Final splat comparison | Five | 5 s | At most 70°C before each round |
| Final Hi-Z no-occlusion calibration | Six, both orders | 5 s | At most 70°C before each repeat |
| Final ray-traced shadows | Five, alternating path/API order | 5 s | At most 70°C before each round |
| Final TAA/GTAO | Three | None | No per-round cooling |

Startup can briefly lower GPU clocks on this laptop, so settled and unsettled suites must remain
separate. The GPU can warm during a round. Radeon temperature was not available. LOD, PT, NRC
and SHaRC were not repeated on the final renderer build; their ratios describe the earlier build.

## Default choices and final measurements

| Feature | Current choice | Evidence and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Windows backend | D3D12 by owner choice | RTX Vulkan can be faster; Metal remains the macOS default |
| Splat rasterizer | Quads on every adapter | Tiles win selected high-overlap views, lose 9–47% at the default garden view |
| Hi-Z | Auto, 200,000 + 0.5 triangles per instance, 1.5× probe bar | Settled no-occlusion calibration costs 0.74–0.92 of the bar |
| AA/AO, discrete Vulkan/D3D12 | MSAA plus depth-derived GTAO | Thin geometry and motion quality; TAA cost is close |
| AA/AO, integrated Vulkan/D3D12 | TAA, GTAO off | TAA saves 0.08–4.77 ms; GTAO adds material cost |
| AA/AO, Metal, browser and other adapters | MSAA, GTAO off | Comparative cost remains unmeasured here |
| Mesh LOD | Enabled within its supported path | Large synthetic field wins; RT shadows require full mesh geometry |
| Ray-traced shadows | Opt-in; cascades default | Small scenes can win, large RTX scenes cost more |
| Neural textures | Explicit `.ntex` opt-in | Storage savings exchange fidelity and per-pixel decode work |

The synthetic LOD ratios reflect deliberately expensive full geometry, not a whole-game speedup.
Neural f32 is faster than f16 on Radeon D3D12: added 1080p decode falls from 4.41 to 3.61 ms.
On browser Radeon, the draw-path readings form overlapping clusters; they do not establish a
reliable general advantage. Median-only comparisons without the spread would hide that limit.

## Limits that affect interpretation

- Apple tile, neural and comparative AA costs were not measured in this Windows work. The older
  loaded Apple splat runs are not a quiet cross-platform baseline.
- RTX Vulkan opaque passes can form distinct timing clusters. Radeon timestamp sums sometimes
  exceed wall time. Hi-Z's no-occlusion margin is thin on Radeon D3D12; inspect repeat ranges.
- Browser neural decode was not repeated in the final quiet work. A Chrome shader check proves
  compatibility, not timing or equivalent image quality across devices.
- Path tracing, NRC and SHaRC remain research paths. Earlier quiet NRC measurements cost more
  than the uncached tracer; they do not justify a default performance claim.
- These fixtures do not compare complete Amoris games with Unreal, Unity, Godot or Bevy. Driver,
  power, resolution, geometry and pass selection constrain every reported ratio.
